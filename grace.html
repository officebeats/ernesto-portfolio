Case Study

Grace
a multi-agent AI system for patient access.

One steering agent routes each patient request to a specialized agent with a defined contract — book, verify, cancel, change, answer — on Google Cloud and Vertex AI.

  • Designed and led by Ernesto Rodriguez — AI Product Lead, Ascension Health, Nov 2024 to Apr 2026
  • Scaled to live volume across the contact center after my tenure — press coverage below
Becker's coverage ↗ How I evaluate agents Email me
Healthcare · 2024–2026 Agentic AI GCP · Vertex AI

Overview

Scale turns small failure rates
into real harm.

  • Designed at Ascension Health — 95 hospitals, $25.3B revenue in fiscal 2025
  • High-volume scheduling and support calls, every day
  • Requests range from simple (confirm, cancel) to complex (new primary care appointments)
  • At that scale, a small failure rate is a large number of patients
  • Design priority: control and review ahead of automation rate

The Challenge

Patients waited.
Staff repeated the same work.

  • Demand outran the team — scheduling and support volume exceeded what human teams could absorb
  • Simple calls crowded out complex ones
  • Inconsistent handling hurt the patient experience
  • Not a "replace the call center" problem — a routing and trust problem
  • The core question: which requests can an agent close safely, and which must reach a person fast — with a clear record either way?

The system

One steering agent.
Many atomic agents.

The steering agent decides

Routes each request to a specialist agent — or a human — before anyone answers.

Atomic agents act

One job each: book · verify · cancel · change · answer. Narrow, testable, replaceable.

Contracts, not prompts

A2A cards state what each agent accepts, returns, and must not do. Reviewable before it reaches a patient.

Built on GCP and Vertex AI

Google Cloud + Vertex AI — HIPAA-compliant, on a unified patient record harmonized across many EHRs.

Architecture

Control first.
Automation second.

  • Narrow agents fail in narrow ways — a booking agent cannot drift into medical advice; governable beats general
  • Reviewable before patients see it — contracts are read, tested, and approved in advance, not discovered in production
  • Every interaction leaves a trace — which agent acted, under which contract, with what result
  • Humans stay in the loop by design — built in, not a fallback; the wrong requests never close alone

Quality & Safety

Every answer had to
earn its way out.

Voice of the customer

Reviews on a set cadence fed design changes — prioritization stayed grounded in patient feedback.

A "do not answer" path

Low-confidence or out-of-scope cases route to humans. Never a guess.

Traceability

Every interaction reviewable after the fact: which agent acted, under which contract, with what result.

Engineering perspective

Madhav Srinivasan, the engineering lead who built GRACE, documents the same system from the engineering side — a HIPAA-compliant voice agent for patient scheduling, built by an AI squad that grew from 2 to 25. The routing design above is what he calls the thinker-responder pattern: a fast, small model picks the specialist agent, then a larger model writes the reply.

My Evaluation Approach

Confidence gates and
a human-review path.

  • One pattern across my work: score the output, gate on confidence
  • Below the bar: the case goes to a human — never a forced answer
  • In production: Grace routes low-confidence cases to people; CleanQuest applies the same pattern at project scale

Verified only at confidence ≥ 0.6. Below: NOT SCORED → human review.

Design Decisions

Decisions
that held up.

Routing before generation. Decide who should handle a request before anyone answers it.

Contracts over prompts. Behavior written down, reviewable, and testable per agent.

A human path by design — not as a fallback after failure. Result: the system scaled to live volume after my tenure, without a redesign.
