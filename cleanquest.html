The problem

Cleaning work
needs a record.

Commercial cleaning employs crews across the US, often with little feedback, recognition, or proof of what they finished.

When a company loses an account over “they didn't clean,” it may have no shared record to settle the dispute. CleanQuest gives the work a visible, reviewable record.

One cleaning run Check in → Clean for 90 seconds → Get a verified score

Business model

Free for cleaners. Funded by sponsors.

CleanQuest is free for cleaners, forever. Sponsors fund the game: brands pay $15K–$55K per package, one sponsor per category, sold through associations and trade shows. Companies pay to use an earned credential commercially — never to earn it. The record a cleaner earns travels with them from job to job, which is why the business model is a trust decision, not just a pricing one.

Key product decisions

How CleanQuest
scores fairly.

Consent first

Keep Cleaners Out of Model Scoring

Faces are never identified. Scores are never used for discipline. Consent is read aloud before a run. I rejected face recognition and typed fine-print consent: the product verifies work, not people — so consent is read aloud, in the cleaner's own language, before the before photo exists.

Honest failure

Give a Reason When a Photo Can't Score

A NOT SCORED result gives one plain reason—wrong area, person in photo, too dark, glare, camera moved, too far, or no change—and offers a retake. Scores are read-only; nobody can edit them. I rejected always returning a number: a score the system can't stand behind teaches crews to distrust every score.

Money stays separate

Sponsor Funding Stays Out of Scoring

CleanQuest is free for cleaners and funded by sponsors. Sponsors have no say over scores or rankings. I rejected sponsored influence on scores, training answers, and rankings — the moment money can move a score, the record is worthless in a dispute.

For night-shift crews

Controls for Night-Shift Cleaning Crews

Zero typing, 44px glove-friendly controls, and English, Mexican Spanish, or Polish. JSON/CSV inspection records travel with a cleaner from job to job.

How it works

Two score reviews.

On device · works offline

MediaPipe EfficientDet-Lite0 and Pose Landmarker Lite run with 96×72 edge analysis and a 3×3 luminance-zone check, including when there is no signal.

Cloud · Second Review

Muse Spark Open runs through a serverless proxy: Baseten is primary, Fireworks AI is failover, and SAM 3.1 outlines surfaces. The app ships with 715 pre-rendered neural voice clips for an offline host.

Score Components

The final score is Clean 50%, Technique 25%, and Time 25%. Clean is computed on-device by spot type: 55% surface, 25% zones, and 20% items by default.

Evaluation & outcomes

How I checked the model.

0.6 Confidence Gate

Cloud surface grades (clean / partly clean / missed) count as verified only at confidence ≥ 0.6. Below 0.6, surfaces display as unverified — and if a run can't be judged fairly, it resolves to NOT SCORED instead of a number.

Score 98 · 1,405 tokens — Primary

In a live verification run, both providers graded the same fixed before/after demo pair (task: vision scorecard). Baseten, the primary, returned an overall score of 98/100 using 1,405 tokens. That is a score the model assigned — not an accuracy rate. It proves the primary path returns a valid, complete scorecard live.

Score 96 · 1,695 tokens — Failover

On the same pair, Fireworks AI, the failover, returned 96/100 using 1,695 tokens — close enough to trust the failover path, different enough to prove it is a real second opinion, not a copy.

The full evaluation write-up — rubric, gate, live run, and refusal design: Evals, run like a product.

Self-Tests: 6/6 · 11/11

Labeled honestly as self-tests: 6/6 hermetic in-app self-tests (no network — provider routing, no-key fallbacks, demo-mode hermeticity, run-log rendering) and 11/11 end-to-end persona walkthroughs in the browser test suite. They verify the software keeps its promises; they are not user research.

What didn't work

What didn't work.

It started crude. CleanQuest began as a local-only proof of concept — local voice, local image recognition, no cloud scoring. The on-device rung still can't judge everything: darkness, glare, a moved camera, or the wrong room all defeat fair grading.

So failure became a designed outcome, not an error state. The NOT SCORED taxonomy — seven plain-language reasons — exists because early runs produced confident scores for photos that deserved none.

One cloud provider wasn't enough. A verification step that depends on a single provider fails when that provider does, so the Fireworks AI failover was built — and both paths were verified live before I trusted either.
